# FIE402 Corporate Finance · Mock exam 4

180 minutes · 100 points · 6 exercises · no multiple choice · calculator and one bilingual dictionary permitted. The formula sheet is handed out. Answer in English and show your calculations.

## Exercise 1 (12 points) · Security issues

Glomfjord Batteri ASA, a listed battery producer with almost no debt and equity worth NOK 8 billion, announces a seasoned equity offering of NOK 1.2 billion to build a new factory. On the announcement day its share price falls by 12% while the market is flat.

**a)** (8 points) What are the potential reasons for the price fall? Describe two distinct reasons, each worth 4 points. Reasons must be other than «the new shares dilute the existing shareholders». Around five sentences in total should be enough.

**b)** (4 points) Suggest a better way to raise the money and explain briefly why the market would react less negatively. (2-3 sentences.)

## Exercise 2 (12 points) · Governance

Kvitstein Medical, a listed company with dispersed ownership, pays its CEO a cash bonus of about twice the salary if earnings per share (EPS) reach a target set by the board. With one quarter left, EPS was heading just below the target. The company then postponed two clinical trials, cutting R&D spending by 30%. It also bought back 6% of its shares with cash meant for a new laboratory. EPS ended just above the target. The next spring the share price fell when two product launches were delayed.

Answer each question in 2-3 sentences (4 points each).

**(i)** Name the problem and explain why the two actions raised EPS without creating value.

**(ii)** Propose one change to the CEO's contract that removes this incentive and one way it can backfire.

**(iii)** Propose one mechanism outside the CEO's pay that could have stopped both actions: who performs it, how it works and one weakness.

## Exercise 3 (18 points) · Capital Structure

Nordfôr ASA, a fish-feed producer, is all-equity financed with 24 million shares and EBIT of NOK 192 million a year in perpetuity. Capital expenditure equals depreciation, working capital is constant and all earnings are paid out. The tax rate is 25%, the equity beta 0.8, the risk-free rate 4% and the expected market return 9%. Markets are perfect apart from taxes. Use 4 decimal places.

**a)** (3 points) Find Nordfôr's unlevered cost of capital, its market value and its share price.

**b)** (3 points) Nordfôr announces that it will borrow a fixed, permanent amount of risk-free debt at 4% to repurchase shares, set so that debt will be 40% of firm value after the repurchase. How much will it borrow? What is the present value of the interest tax shield, at which rate and why?

**c)** (4 points) What happens to the share price on the announcement? How many shares are repurchased and at what price? Who gains more: sellers or keepers?

**d)** (4 points) What is the expected dividend per share after the recapitalisation? What is the new equity cost of capital?

**e)** (4 points) The CFO notes that permanent debt adds value through the tax shield and proposes debt of 90% of firm value. She argues that bankruptcy costs are small: legal fees are about 3% of firm value and the annual bankruptcy probability is below 1%. Nordfôr's specialised plants can only be sold to other feed producers, hit by the same downturns. Explain briefly, with reference to fire sales, why her argument understates the cost of high leverage. (3-5 sentences.)

## Exercise 4 (18 points) · Valuation with leverage

Hjeltnes Transport ASA, a trucking company, is offered a three-year contract to run a plastics sorting plant. The project has the risk of Ren Sortering ASA, a listed firm that only runs such plants. Hjeltnes has already paid NOK 1.5 million for an engineering study. Figures are in NOK million.

The equipment costs 60 today (Year 0) and is depreciated straight-line to zero over Years 1-3; it is worthless afterwards. Revenue and cash operating costs:

| Year | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 62 | 72 | 68 |
| Operating costs (cash) | 34 | 38 | 37 |

Net working capital is 6.0, 7.5 and 7.0 at the end of Years 0, 1 and 2 and is fully recovered at the end of Year 3. The tax rate is 22%.

Ren Sortering: market capitalisation 960, risk-free debt 400, cash 180 (60 needed in operations, the rest excess), equity beta 1.24. The risk-free rate is 4% and the expected market return 9%.

Both firms keep their leverage constant: the project's D/E is 0.5 (rebalanced at each year end) and its debt costs 5.5%. Use 4 decimal places.

**a)** (3 points) Find the project's free cash flow in Years 0 to 3.

**b)** (3 points) Find the project's unlevered cost of capital.

**c)** (2 points) Find the project's equity cost of capital and after-tax WACC.

**d)** (4 points) Find the project's NPV with the WACC method and its debt capacity at the end of Years 0-2.

**e)** (3 points) Value the project with APV (unlevered value, yearly tax shields, PV(TS)) and compare with d).

**f)** (3 points) Why is the after-tax WACC lower than r_U? A colleague says debt is simply cheaper than equity. Is she right? (2-3 sentences.)

## Exercise 5 (20 points) · Real Options

You consider opening a bakery-café. The fit-out costs NOK 600,000 now and has no resale value; running costs are NOK 41,000 a month. Monthly revenue will be NOK 36,000 or NOK 51,000 forever, with equal probability; you learn which right after opening. Cash flows arrive at the end of each month. Demand is unrelated to the market, so discount at the risk-free rate of 6% a year. The standard lease runs forever and cannot be terminated. Give the monthly rate in percent with 4 decimals (as in 0.1234%) and use it throughout.

**a)** (2 points) What is the monthly rate? What is the NPV of opening now under the standard lease? Will you open?

**b)** (4 points) The landlord offers instead a lease you can terminate at no cost at the end of month 24, after which you receive nothing. What is the NPV of opening now? Will you open?

**c)** (4 points) How much is the right to terminate after 24 months worth to you, given what you would do without it? How much more would it be worth with termination at the end of month 12?

**d)** (4 points) Return to the lease in b). The landlord now charges a fee F, paid at the end of month 24 only if you leave. What is the highest F at which you would still open?

**e)** (6 points) Without a fee, let the probability of high revenue be p. For which p are you indifferent about opening with the lease in b)? Under the standard lease? Explain briefly why the two differ.

## Exercise 6 (20 points) · M&A

Gaustad Energi ASA (A) bids for Bølgefiber ASA (B), offering 3 A shares for every 2 B shares. The bid was a surprise. The deal will be completed or fail in exactly 3 months; if it fails, both prices return to their pre-announcement levels, which reflect stand-alone values.

Before the announcement: 150 million A shares at P_{A,0} = NOK 40.00 and 30 million B shares at P_{B,0} = NOK 50.00. Just after: P_{A,1} = NOK 41.70 and P_{B,1} = NOK 59.60. The risk-free rate is 4.5% a year. Use 4 decimal places.

**a)** (3 points) What is the offer worth per B share at the new prices? What is the spread in NOK and in percent of B's price? Why does B trade below the offer?

**b)** (9 points) Calculate the probability of completion perceived by the market. (Hint: use a long-short strategy, invest the short-sale proceeds at the risk-free rate and remember that the deal is decided in 3 months.)

**c)** (4 points) If the deal succeeds, the merged company will be worth the two pre-announcement market values plus synergies S. What S do the prices after the announcement imply? Use the assumptions of b).

**d)** (4 points) What if the decision is delayed by one year, to 15 months? How would the calculation in b) change? What do you expect for B's share price and why? (3-4 sentences, no calculation needed.)
