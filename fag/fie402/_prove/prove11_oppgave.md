# FIE402 Corporate Finance · Mock exam 11

180 minutes · 100 points · 6 exercises · no multiple choice · calculator and one bilingual dictionary permitted. The formula sheet is handed out. Show every calculation.

## Exercise 1: Capital Structure (12 points)

The CFO of a listed car-parts maker makes three claims:

(i) «If we borrow to buy back 20% of our shares, earnings per share will rise. Higher EPS means a higher share price, so the buyback creates value.»

(ii) «Our profits swing with the price of steel. Hedging the steel price with futures will make the company more valuable.»

(iii) «Our debt costs 5% and our equity 11%. Replacing equity with debt will lower our cost of capital.»

For each claim: is it right in a perfect capital market? Name one market imperfection that could make it right and explain how (2-3 sentences per claim, 4 points each).

## Exercise 2: Governance (12 points)

Westbrook Foods Inc. is a listed company with dispersed ownership: no shareholder owns more than 1%. The board, mostly picked by the CEO, has approved all his proposals. The CEO uses the company jet for private trips and has moved the head office into an expensive new tower. Profits have twice been restated after questions about how revenue was booked. The shares trade about 25% below analysts' estimate of what the business would be worth under better management.

The shareholders are too dispersed to act and the board will not. Explain how each of the following could discipline the CEO and give one limitation: (a) the company's lenders, (b) employees or regulators, (c) the market for corporate control (2-3 sentences each, 4 points each).

## Exercise 3: Asymmetric information (18 points)

Brekke Sensor ASA has 40 million shares, no debt and NOK 200 million of excess cash. Contract news will arrive shortly. If it is good, the value of Brekke's business (its enterprise value) will be NOK 2,000 million; if it is bad, NOK 1,000 million. Investors put the probability of good news at 40% and value the business at its expected value. Management already knows the news. All the excess cash will be used to repurchase shares at the market price, either now or after the news. Ignore taxes and discounting. Use 4 decimal places.

**a) (3 points)** What is Brekke's share price today?

**b) (3 points)** Brekke repurchases shares now, before the news; investors do not yet read anything into the timing. How many shares does it buy? What is the share price after good news and after bad news?

**c) (4 points)** Brekke instead repurchases after the news is out. What is the share price after the repurchase in each case?

**d) (4 points)** Management acts for the shareholders who keep their shares. When does it repurchase if the news is good? When if it is bad? If the news is good, who gains and who loses from this choice? How much?

**e) (4 points)** Now investors know how management times repurchases. What happens to the share price when Brekke announces an immediate repurchase? Given this reaction, would management announce one if the news were bad? (3-4 sentences.)

## Exercise 4: Valuation with leverage (18 points)

A private-equity fund asks you to value Rondane Sport AS, an unlisted chain of sports shops, which it will buy now (Year 0). The fund will cut inventory; other working capital stays constant. The tax rate is 22%. Projections:

| NOK million | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 400 | 440 | 470 |
| Cost of goods sold | 250 | 268 | 280 |
| Other operating costs | 50 | 52 | 55 |
| Depreciation | 40 | 40 | 40 |
| Capital expenditure | 50 | 45 | 40 |
| Inventory at year end (Year 0: 120) | 105 | 95 | 95 |
| Interest on the current loan | 18 | 18 | 18 |

From Year 4 on, free cash flow grows by 2% a year forever. After the purchase Rondane will keep debt at 40% of firm value (rebalanced yearly) and borrow at 6%. Fjellsport ASA, a listed retailer with the same business risk, has an equity beta of 1.3, a constant debt-to-equity ratio of 0.5 and a cost of debt of 5.5%. The risk-free rate is 4% and the expected market return 10%.

Each question is worth 2 points. Recommended precision is 4 decimal places.

**a) (2 points)** Find Rondane's free cash flow in Years 1 to 3.

**b) (2 points)** Find Fjellsport's asset beta and Rondane's unlevered cost of capital.

**c) (2 points)** Find Rondane's after-tax WACC.

**d) (2 points)** Find Rondane's terminal value at the end of Year 3 (WACC method).

**e) (2 points)** Find Rondane's levered value today (WACC method).

**f) (2 points)** Find Rondane's debt at the end of Years 0 and 1. How much debt is issued or repaid at the end of Year 1?

**g) (2 points)** Find Rondane's unlevered value today.

**h) (2 points)** Find the interest tax shields in Years 1 to 4 and the present value of all tax shields.

**i) (2 points)** Find Rondane's levered value today by APV and compare with e).

## Exercise 5: Real Options (20 points)

Djuphav Mineral AS can buy a seabed-mining vessel for NOK 240 million. Its cash flows start one year after the purchase and depend on a court case. With probability p = 10%, an environmental group sues this year to stop seabed mining. If it sues, the court then stops all mining with probability 60% (cash flows 0 forever); otherwise the case is dismissed and the industry expands (NOK 120 million per year forever). If nobody sues, the cash flows are NOK 48 million per year forever. The outcome is known before the first cash flow. Discount all cash flows at 8%.

**a) (3 points)** What is the NPV of buying the vessel today?

**b) (5 points)** Instead, Djuphav can wait one year, learn the outcome and then decide whether to buy for the same NOK 240 million (cash flows then start in Year 2). What is the NPV today of waiting? Is it better or worse than buying today? Explain the intuition.

**c) (5 points)** At which probability p of a lawsuit (all else unchanged) are you indifferent between buying today and waiting?

**d) (4 points)** From now on p = 30%. If Djuphav buys today, it can sell the vessel at the end of Year 1 for NOK 180 million in any state, giving up all later cash flows. What is the NPV of buying today? Do you buy today or wait?

**e) (3 points)** Explain the intuition behind c) and d): how do the probability of a lawsuit and the right to sell the vessel affect the advantage of waiting over buying today? (2-3 sentences.)

## Exercise 6: M&A (20 points)

Havstad Seafood ASA (A) has made a surprise offer for Smoltvik ASA (B): 4 A shares for every 5 B shares. The deal will be completed or fail in exactly 6 months; if it fails, both share prices return to their pre-announcement levels. Before the announcement P_A,0 = NOK 50.00 and P_B,0 = NOK 30.00; just after it P_A,1 = NOK 52.00 and P_B,1 = NOK 39.00. The risk-free rate is 4% a year. Use 4 decimal places.

**a) (4 points)** What is the offer worth per B share at the old and at the new prices? Why does B trade below the offer?

**b) (8 points)** Calculate the probability of completion perceived by the market. (Hint: use a long-short strategy.)

**c) (4 points)** A's shareholders vote on the deal. Using P_A,1 and p from b), what will an A share be worth at completion if the deal goes through? How do you vote as a shareholder of A? Briefly explain.

**d) (4 points)** One month after the announcement the vote result is published: A now trades at NOK 53.00 and B at NOK 42.00, with the deal still decided at the 6-month date. What was the outcome of the vote? What do the prices tell you about the probability of completion? (3-4 sentences; no full calculation needed.)
