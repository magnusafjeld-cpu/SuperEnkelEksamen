# FIE402 Corporate Finance · Mock exam 8

180 minutes · 100 points · calculator and one bilingual dictionary · formula sheet handed out.

## Exercise 1: Security issues (12 points)

Lysaker Robotics ASA went public last year at NOK 50 a share; the shares closed at NOK 58 on the first day. Three years later the stock has returned less than comparable firms. Both patterns are typical of IPOs.

a) Explain the winner's curse and why it makes issuers underprice IPOs. (2-3 sentences.)

b) Give two other explanations for IPO underpricing. (1-2 sentences each.)

c) Why do recent IPOs tend to underperform comparable firms in the following years? (2-3 sentences.)

Each part is worth 4 points.

## Exercise 2: Governance (12 points)

At the annual meeting of Fjordmat ASA, a listed food producer, shareholders propose to change the board's mandate from maximising shareholder value to balancing the interests of shareholders, employees, customers, local communities and the environment.

a) Give one argument for keeping shareholder value as the objective and one argument for the proposal. (6 points; 2-3 sentences per argument.)

b) Suppose the proposal is adopted. Explain one problem it creates for how the board evaluates and rewards the CEO and one way the board can limit it. (6 points; 3-4 sentences.)

## Exercise 3: Capital Structure (18 points)

Skagerak Drone ASA is all-equity financed with 12 million shares. Next year its assets will be worth NOK 240, 190, 120 or 80 million with equal probability. The risk is diversifiable; r_f = 5%. In default, 30% of the asset value is lost to legal costs and a fire sale. There are no taxes. Use 4 decimal places.

**a) (3 points)** What is Skagerak worth today and what is its share price?

**b) (3 points)** Skagerak plans to issue zero-coupon debt with face value NOK 140 million due next year and use the proceeds to repurchase shares. Shares are repurchased at the market price after the announcement. What will the debt and the equity be worth after the transaction?

**c) (4 points)** What are the yield to maturity and the expected return on the debt? Explain briefly why they differ.

**d) (4 points)** What happens to the share price when the plan is announced? How many shares does Skagerak repurchase?

**e) (4 points)** Who bears the expected costs of financial distress? If interest were tax deductible, how large would the present value of the tax shield have to be for the plan to benefit the shareholders? (2-3 sentences.)

## Exercise 4: Valuation (18 points)

Bergfrukt ASA, a juice producer, considers a line of oat drinks. Figures are in NOK million; the tax rate is 22%.

- Bergfrukt has already paid 1.2 for a market study.
- The line costs 30, paid when it is delivered and installed at the end of Year 1. It produces in Years 2-4 and is depreciated straight-line to zero over those years.
- The oat drinks will cut the cash margin on Bergfrukt's juices by 4 a year in Years 2-4.
- The line needs an empty warehouse Bergfrukt owns, which could instead be leased out from today for four years (no shorter lease) at 2.5 a year before tax, paid at the end of Years 1-4; discount the fixed rent at 5%. Ignore the warehouse after the line or lease ends.
- Bergfrukt is all-equity financed (cost of capital 7%) and finances the line with equity. Havre ASA, a listed pure oat-drink producer, has an equity beta of 1.35, a constant D/E of 0.25 and risk-free debt. r_f = 4%; the expected market return is 9%.

| Year | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue |  | 40 | 50 | 45 |
| Operating costs (cash) |  | 18 | 22 | 20 |
| Net working capital, end of year | 4.8 | 6 | 5.4 | 0 |

**a) (3 points)** What discount rate should Bergfrukt use for the line? Why not 7%?

**b) (5 points)** Find the free cash flow of the line in Years 1 to 4, leaving out the warehouse.

**c) (3 points)** What is the NPV of the project today?

**d) (4 points)** For the rest of the exercise, another tenant offers an 18-month lease that pays NOK 3 million before tax at t = 1.5. The line would then be delivered half a year later: every cash flow in b) comes six months later. What is the NPV of this plan today, measured as in c)? Is it better than the plan in c) and by how much?

**e) (3 points)** The CEO tells the board: «The line's cash inflows are worth more than it costs, so Bergfrukt, now worth NOK 900 million, will be worth more with it. And the oat drinks bring NOK 135 million of new revenue.» What is wrong with this reasoning and what should the board decide on? (3-4 sentences.)

## Exercise 5: Real Options (20 points)

Lysne Vaksine AS can develop a cod vaccine in two stages. Lab trials cost NOK 7 million today and succeed with probability 30%, known at t = 1. Field trials then cost NOK 44 million at t = 1 and succeed with probability 60%, known at t = 2. If both succeed, the vaccine is worth NOK 260 million at t = 2; otherwise nothing. The risks are not related to the market; r_f = 5%.

**a) (3 points)** A developer offers to run both stages for a fixed price of NOK 45 million paid today. What is the NPV of this all-at-once contract?

**b) (5 points)** Instead, Lysne can pay for each stage itself and decide at t = 1 whether to run the field trials. What is the NPV of staged financing?

**c) (4 points)** How much more is staged financing worth than the contract in a)? What is the highest price of the all-at-once contract at which Lysne would choose it?

**d) (3 points)** Suppose instead the lab trials succeed with probability 20% and the field trials then with 90% (same costs, same overall chance). What is the NPV of staged financing now? Explain the change (1-2 sentences).

**e) (5 points)** Return to the original probabilities. The developer adds a clause: Lysne may cancel the all-at-once contract at t = 1 and get NOK 40 million back. Which financing should Lysne choose? How does the clause change the value of committing early? (2-3 sentences.)

## Exercise 6: M&A (20 points)

Nordlaks ASA and Fjellkraft ASA have these asset values next year (NOK million):

| State | Probability | Nordlaks | Fjellkraft |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 25% | 180 | 60 |
| 2 | 50% | 120 | 90 |
| 3 | 25% | 40 | 130 |

Nordlaks has zero-coupon debt with face value NOK 100 million due next year, Fjellkraft NOK 75 million. Investors are risk neutral and the risk-free rate is 0%, so no discounting is needed. There are no taxes or default costs. The firms plan to merge without synergies; the merged firm takes over both loans, which rank equally.

**a) (3 points)** What are the debt and the equity of each firm worth before the merger?

**b) (8 points)** What are the merged firm's debt and equity worth? What is each loan worth after the merger? What is the yield to maturity on Nordlaks' loan before and after?

**c) (5 points)** Who wins and who loses from the merger and by how much? Is diversification a good motive for the merger from the shareholders' point of view? (2-3 sentences.)

**d) (4 points)** For the rest of the exercise: Torvik ASA (net income NOK 60 million, 20 million shares, price NOK 45) buys Lunde ASA (net income NOK 16 million, 6 million shares, price NOK 24) in a share swap at Lunde's market price, without synergies. What happens to Torvik's EPS? Is value created for Torvik's shareholders? Explain briefly.
