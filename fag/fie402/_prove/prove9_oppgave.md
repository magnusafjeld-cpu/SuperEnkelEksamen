# FIE402 Corporate Finance · Mock exam 9

180 minutes · 100 points · 6 exercises · no multiple choice · calculator and one bilingual dictionary permitted. The formula sheet is handed out. Show every calculation.

## Exercise 1: Payout policy (12 points)

Hestnes Byggevarer, a mature listed company, has paid a dividend of NOK 10 per share for twelve years. It now cuts the dividend to NOK 4 and will spend the cash saved on share buybacks every year. Investment plans and earnings forecasts are unchanged.

a) In perfect capital markets, is a shareholder who lives on the NOK 10 dividend worse off and why?

b) The CFO expects the price to rise, since buybacks signal undervaluation; the CEO fears a fall. Who is more likely to be right and why?

c) In this exercise the shareholders are individuals (taxed 35% on dividends, 20% on realised gains), tax-exempt pension funds and companies taxed lightly on dividends received but fully on gains. Which group gains, which loses and which does not care? How does the shareholder base change?

2-3 sentences per part, 4 points each.

## Exercise 2: Governance (12 points)

Pemberton Foods Inc., a US-listed firm with dispersed ownership, has lagged its peers for five years. An activist hedge fund with 4% of the shares believes that selling a loss-making division and paying out the proceeds would raise the firm's value by about 20%. The CEO refuses.

a) Describe the fund's steps from its first contact with the board to a proxy contest.

b) Who pays for a proxy contest on each side and why does that favour the incumbents?

c) Every shareholder gains if the campaign succeeds. Explain the resulting free-rider problem and one way the fund can still make the campaign pay.

2-3 sentences per part, 4 points each.

## Exercise 3: Debt and risk (18 points)

Sandøy Rederi AS owns one bulk carrier and no cash. Its bank loan, face value NOK 150 million, is due at t = 1, when the assets will be worth NOK 250 million if freight rates are high (probability 40%) and NOK 90 million if low; if Sandøy cannot repay in full, the bank takes the assets. Investors are risk neutral, the risk-free rate is 0% and management acts for the existing shareholders.

A retrofit costing NOK 30 million today would raise the value of the assets at t = 1 by NOK 45 million in both states.

**a) (2 points)** Find the values of the loan and the equity today, without the retrofit.

**b) (4 points)** The 30 can only be raised with new shares sold at a fair price, giving new investors a fraction x of the equity. Find x. Will the existing shareholders approve the retrofit?

**c) (4 points)** Find the NPV of the retrofit and its effect on the values of the loan and of the existing shareholders' stake. Who gains and why?

**d) (4 points)** The bank offers to cut the face value to 125 if the retrofit goes ahead, financed as in b). Do the existing shareholders approve? What do they and the bank gain compared with a)?

**e) (4 points)** Return to the face value of 150. If the bank waived its covenant against senior debt, Sandøy could borrow the 30 from a new senior lender. Would the shareholders then retrofit and would the bank agree? Why do loan contracts contain such covenants?

## Exercise 4: Valuation (18 points)

A private-equity fund plans to buy Nordvarme AS, a district-heating firm with EBIT of NOK 60 million a year forever; capital expenditure equals depreciation and working capital is constant. The tax rate is 22% and analysts put the unlevered cost of capital at 8%. After the purchase Nordvarme will have NOK 250 million of permanent debt at the risk-free rate of 4%. The expected market return is 9%.

**a) (2 points)** Find Nordvarme's free cash flow and its all-equity value.

**b) (4 points)** Find PV(TS) with the rate and why. Find Nordvarme's levered value by APV and its equity value.

**c) (4 points)** The fund pays NOK 600 million for the shares, using the new debt and NOK 350 million of its own money. Find its NPV and say who captures the tax shield.

**d) (4 points)** Two listed firms with the same business risk keep constant debt-to-equity ratios. Fjordvarme ASA: equity NOK 1,000 million, risk-free debt NOK 500 million, equity beta 1.26. Kystvarme ASA: equity NOK 1,200 million, debt NOK 300 million with beta 0.2, equity beta 0.9. Estimate r_U from both firms. Is the analysts' 8% consistent?

**e) (4 points)** Suppose Nordvarme instead kept debt at a constant fraction of firm value, rebalanced yearly. Without calculations, would the tax shield be worth more or less than in b) and why?

## Exercise 5: Options (20 points)

Havgløtt Vind AS owns one offshore wind farm, worth NOK 500 million today and NOK 620 million (high power prices) or NOK 400 million (low) in one year. Its only debt is a one-year zero-coupon bond with face value NOK 450 million; no dividend is paid before it matures. The risk-free rate is 4%. Treat the farm as a traded asset in a perfect market. Analysts put the probability of high prices at 65%. Use 4 decimal places.

**a) (3 points)** Find the risk-neutral probability of the high state. How does it compare with the analysts' 65% and why?

**b) (8 points)** Value the equity as a call on the farm by replicating its payoff with the farm and risk-free bonds. Then value the debt as farm minus equity and as risk-free debt minus a put on the farm, priced by put-call parity.

**c) (5 points)** Find the promised yield, the credit spread and the default probability implied by the debt's price. Find the expected return on the debt under the analysts' probabilities and explain the gap to the yield.

**d) (4 points)** Management can cancel the farm's fixed-price power contract for a fee and sell at spot prices. Treat the switched farm as a new traded asset, worth NOK 490 million after the fee and NOK 700 million or NOK 300 million in one year. Find the new values of equity and debt. Would the shareholders want the switch and why?

## Exercise 6: Real Options (20 points)

You hold an exclusive right to build a truck-charging hub for NOK 60 million. From one year after it is built, it pays NOK 6 million a year forever if demand is high and NOK 2.7 million if low (equal probability). Demand is revealed at the end of Year 1 whether you have built or not and never changes. Demand risk is not systematic: discount at the risk-free rate of 6%. You can build now or at the end of Year 1; then the right expires. Use 4 decimal places.

**a) (3 points)** What is the NPV of building now?

**b) (5 points)** Find the NPV today of waiting a year to decide and the value of this flexibility over building now. Do you build now or wait?

**c) (4 points)** If you have not built by the end of Year 1, a rival opens next to you then with probability 30% (independent of demand), before you decide; your cash flows would then fall to 70%. Building now keeps the rival out. What is the NPV of waiting? Do you build now or wait?

**d) (4 points)** For which entry probability are you indifferent between building now and waiting? Why does the rival make waiting less attractive?

**e) (4 points)** Ignore the rival. If your right lasted two years, would it be worth more, less or the same? Explain briefly (2-4 sentences).
