# FIE402 Corporate Finance · Mock exam 7

180 minutes · 100 points · 6 exercises · no multiple choice · calculator and one bilingual dictionary permitted. The formula sheet is handed out. Show every calculation.

## Exercise 1: Options (12 points)

Kvitholmen Shipping ASA has assets worth V and one zero-coupon bond with face value K due in three years. It pays no dividends. Answer without calculations: 2-3 sentences per question, 4 points each.

a) Would American call and put options on Kvitholmen's shares be worth more, less or the same as European ones with the same strike and maturity? Answer for both.

b) Why can the bond be viewed as a risk-free bond minus a put? Name the underlying and the strike and say what the put means for the shareholders.

c) Kvitholmen replaces its long-term charters with spot-market trading. Its assets become more volatile, but V is unchanged. What happens to the equity and to the bond? Who wins and who loses?

## Exercise 2: Governance (12 points)

Larkspur Logistics Inc. has dispersed ownership. Its shares have lagged its peers for four years; last spring a private equity fund approached the board about a takeover. The board now asks the annual meeting to approve two charter changes:

(1) a staggered board: three-year terms, with one third of the seats up for election each year;  
(2) a merger or the removal of a director needs the votes of 75% of all shares outstanding.

The CEO supports both: «A manager who must fear for the job every quarter cannot invest for the long run.»

a) How does change (1) protect the managers and what does it cost the shareholders?  
b) The same for change (2).  
c) When can protecting managers from monitoring raise firm value? How would you vote and how should the share price react if both changes pass?

4 points per question; 2-3 sentences each.

## Exercise 3: Security issues (18 points)

Brattholm Salmon ASA has 40 million shares and no debt. At t = 1 its existing assets will be worth NOK 1,050 million (high type) or NOK 700 million (low type). The CEO knows the type; investors only know that the high type has probability 40%. Brattholm can invest NOK 180 million now in a plant that pays a certain NOK 200 million at t = 1, or lose the chance. Investors know about the plant. Unless stated otherwise, it must be financed by selling new shares at the market price to competitive, risk-neutral investors. The CEO maximises the value of the existing shares once the type is known. The risk-free rate is 0%. Use 4 decimal places.

**a) (3 points)** Suppose investors believe that both types issue. At what price are the new shares sold and how many are issued?

**b) (3 points)** On these terms, what is an existing share worth to each type if Brattholm issues? Which type or types issue?

**c) (4 points)** Find the equilibrium issue price. Before the announcement investors know about the plant but not the type. By how much, in percent, does the share price change when Brattholm announces a share issue?

**d) (5 points)** Instead, a bank offers a risk-free loan of NOK 180 million whose covenants cost the existing shareholders NOK 10 million (present value) for either type. Investors know about the offer. What does each type do now? By how much, in percent, does the share price change on each possible announcement?

**e) (3 points)** Use your results to explain why share prices fall on average when firms announce equity issues, even for positive-NPV projects. Which financing should a high-type firm arrange before its next project? (3-4 sentences)

## Exercise 4: Valuation with leverage (18 points)

Saltstraum Kraft ASA has a market capitalisation of NOK 500 million and NOK 820 million of debt costing 5%. It holds NOK 100 million in cash, of which NOK 30 million is needed in operations. Its cost of equity is 12% and its tax rate 22%. It keeps its debt-to-value ratio constant.

A plant upgrade costs NOK 90 million today and gives free cash flows of NOK 50, 65 and 70 million at the end of years 1 to 3, with the firm's risk. Saltstraum will fund it with its excess cash and new debt, keeping the debt-to-value ratio constant; any cash left over goes to the shareholders. Use 4 decimal places.

**a) (3 points)** What is Saltstraum's WACC?

**b) (3 points)** What are the value and the NPV of the upgrade?

**c) (3 points)** What is the upgrade's debt capacity at t = 0, 1, 2 and 3?

**d) (4 points)** How much new debt does Saltstraum issue today and how much is paid out? By how much are the shareholders better off?

**e) (5 points)** Value the upgrade with APV and compare with b). Which rate did you use for the tax shields and why? (1-2 sentences)

## Exercise 5: Options (20 points)

Ridgeline Software Inc. has 400 million shares at USD 90 and no debt. The risk-free rate is 3% a year and markets are perfect. Ridgeline considers issuing zero-coupon debt with face value USD 30 billion due in 15 months and paying the proceeds out to its shareholders at once. Below are quotes for European options on its shares expiring in 15 months; treat them as options on its assets per share. Give yields as annual rates. Use 4 decimal places.

| Strike (USD) | Call bid | Call ask | Put bid | Put ask |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 45 | 46.82 | 47.02 | 0.24 | 0.34 |
| 50 | 42.31 | 42.51 | 0.55 | 0.65 |
| 75 | 23.24 | 23.44 | 5.57 | 5.67 |
| 80 | 20.30 | 20.50 | 7.45 | 7.55 |

**a) (3 points)** What would the equity be worth after the debt issue? Explain which quote you use.

**b) (4 points)** Value the debt once with a call and once with a put. What yield would Ridgeline pay?

**c) (8 points)** Suppose instead the debt is split into USD 20 billion senior and USD 10 billion junior, both zero-coupon and due in 15 months. Find the value of each and the yield on the junior debt.

**d) (5 points)** Write the junior holders' payoff at maturity with options and explain it. If the volatility of Ridgeline's assets rose with their value unchanged, what would happen to the value of the junior debt? (3-4 sentences)

## Exercise 6: M&A (20 points)

Vestby Group ASA (60 million shares at NOK 75) wants to acquire Lundar Foods ASA (25 million shares at NOK 36). Both prices are from before any bid and reflect stand-alone values. Vestby expects synergies with a present value of NOK 300 million. Amounts are in NOK million unless given per share.

**a) (3 points)** Vestby offers NOK 46 in cash per Lundar share. What is the deal's NPV for each group of shareholders?

**b) (4 points)** Alternatively, Vestby offers 0.6 new Vestby shares per Lundar share. What are the two NPVs?

**c) (4 points)** Vestby's managers think their shares are worth only NOK 66. By their valuation, which offer do they prefer? How will the market react to a share offer?

**d) (5 points)** From now on NOK 75 is the correct value of a Vestby share. A rival, Nordkyst ASA, has synergies of NOK 200 million with Lundar. If Nordkyst buys Lundar, Vestby loses NOK 150 million in value; if Vestby buys it, Nordkyst loses NOK 150 million. What is the highest cash price per Lundar share each bidder will pay? Who wins and at roughly what price?

**e) (4 points)** Are Vestby's shareholders better or worse off than before Vestby made any bid? Why do target shareholders capture most of the gains from acquisitions? (3-4 sentences)
