# FIE402 Corporate Finance · Mock exam 5

180 minutes · 100 points · 6 exercises · no multiple choice · calculator and one bilingual dictionary permitted · the course formula sheet is handed out.

Answer in English. Show every calculation.

## Exercise 1: Payout policy (12 points)

Skarvøy Telecom ASA, a mature listed telecom operator with few investment needs, expects about NOK 1.2 billion of free cash flow a year. The board considers four ways to use this year's cash: (1) raise the regular dividend, (2) buy back shares, (3) keep the cash, (4) invest in data centres, a new business area proposed by the CEO. Dividends and capital gains are taxed at the same rate, but a gain is taxed only when the shares are sold. Managers know more than investors and may pursue their own interests.

Give one advantage and one risk of each alternative. (1-2 sentences per alternative. Each alternative is worth 3 points: 1.5 for the advantage and 1.5 for the risk. An argument already used for another alternative earns no points the second time.)

## Exercise 2: Governance (12 points)

Ashgrove Medical Devices Inc. is a US-listed company with dispersed ownership. Its 13 directors are:

- the CEO, who also chairs the board;
- three other executives;
- two directors with business ties: the former CFO and a partner in a law firm paid USD 4 million a year by Ashgrove;
- seven independent directors, four of whom sit on four or more other boards.

The board is staggered (one third elected each year) and nominates its own candidates. The stock has lagged its peers by 40% over five years, yet the CEO keeps his job and pay.

Select three weaknesses of this board. For each, explain why it weakens the monitoring of the CEO and name one remedy for the shareholders. (2-3 sentences each, 4 points: 2 for the why, 2 for the remedy. The executives and the two directors with business ties count as one weakness.)

## Exercise 3: Capital Structure (18 points)

Ravnfjell Elektro ASA operates in a perfect capital market with no taxes. It is all-equity financed with 20 million shares, expects EBIT of NOK 66 million every year forever (no growth) and pays out all earnings as dividends at the end of each year. Its equity beta is 1.4, the risk-free rate is 4% and the expected market return is 9%.

**a)** (3 points) What are the share price and the forward P/E ratio (price divided by next year's expected EPS)?

**b)** (4 points) Ravnfjell borrows NOK 180 million of permanent risk-free debt to buy back shares. What happens to the share price on the announcement? How many shares are bought back? What is the new cost of equity?

**c)** (4 points) What is next year's expected EPS after the transaction? The CFO says the higher EPS shows that the shareholders gain. Do you agree? Explain briefly.

**d)** (4 points) For the rest of the exercise there is no recapitalisation. Instead, Ravnfjell pays NOK 5.10 per share at the end of Year 1 and raises the part not covered by earnings by issuing new shares right after the payment (the new shares do not receive it). Later, all earnings are again paid out. At what price are the new shares issued? How many are needed?

**e)** (3 points) Are the original shareholders better or worse off? What is the share price today, after the announcement? Explain briefly.

## Exercise 4: Valuation with leverage (18 points)

Frostheim Logistics ASA expects EBIT of NOK 50 million in Year 1. Capital expenditure equals depreciation and net working capital rises by NOK 3 million in Year 1; all items then grow at 3% a year forever. The tax rate is 22%, the risk-free rate 3%, the expected market return 9% and the asset beta 1.0.

Frostheim keeps a constant interest coverage ratio: each year's interest is 30% of that year's free cash flow, paid at r_D = 4.5% on the debt outstanding at the start of the year. Use 4 decimal places (rates in percent: 8.1234%).

**a)** (3 points) What is Frostheim's unlevered value V^U?

**b)** (3 points) What is Frostheim's levered value V^L?

**c)** (2 points) How much debt does Frostheim have today? What is its equity worth?

**d)** (4 points) What is Frostheim's cost of equity? Value its equity with the flow-to-equity (FTE) method.

**e)** (3 points) Value the firm with the WACC method.

**f)** (3 points) Explain briefly why r_wacc is lower than r_U. (1-2 sentences)

## Exercise 5: Real Options (20 points)

Vestvik Proteins AS can build a production unit for NOK 48 million. It produces 100,000 kg of protein a year forever at a cost of NOK 20 per kg and cannot be shut down. The first output is sold the moment the unit is built and then once a year. Today's price is NOK 60 per kg; at the end of Year 1 it moves to NOK 90 or NOK 30 with equal probability and stays there. The risk is not systematic: discount at r_f = 8%. No taxes.

**a)** (3 points) What is the NPV of building the unit today?

**b)** (5 points) Vestvik can instead wait a year, observe the price and then decide (same cost; output again starts at once). What is the value of the option to wait? Do you build today or wait?

**c)** (4 points) Suppose the Year-1 price is instead NOK 105 or NOK 15. Without calculations: what happens to the NPV of building today and to the value of waiting? (2-3 sentences)

**d)** (8 points) For the rest of the exercise, consider a new project. A pilot costs NOK 5 million today and shows after one year whether the process works (probability 40%); if it does not, a full-scale unit is worthless. If it works, the full-scale unit costs NOK 30 million at the end of Year 1 and succeeds with probability 50%: it is then worth NOK 120 million at the end of Year 2, otherwise nothing. Same 8% rate.

Compare the NPV of committing to both stages today (the NOK 30 million is paid whatever the pilot shows) with staged financing (paid only if the process works). Explain the difference briefly.

## Exercise 6: M&A (20 points)

Fjordbit ASA (A) offers to acquire Lyngtopp Data ASA (B), paying 0.70 A shares for each B share. A has 50 million shares at NOK 80 and B has 20 million shares at NOK 45 (stand-alone values). A's management estimates the synergies at S = NOK 300 million.

**a)** (4 points) What is the NPV of the deal to A's and to B's shareholders?

**b)** (3 points) What is the highest exchange ratio A's shareholders should accept?

**c)** (6 points) A could instead pay NOK 56 in cash per B share. What is A's NPV? If the synergies turn out to be only NOK 100 million, what is A's NPV with cash and with shares? Who bears the synergy risk under each method?

**d)** (4 points) Back to S = NOK 300 million. A's management believes A's shares are worth NOK 72, not 80. By that valuation, which offer is better for A's shareholders, shares or NOK 56 in cash? By how much? What does the market infer from the choice?

**e)** (3 points) B also has NOK 400 million of risky debt, which the merged firm takes over; A's cash flows are not perfectly correlated with B's. Without synergies, what happens to the value of B's debt? Who gains and who loses? (2-3 sentences)
