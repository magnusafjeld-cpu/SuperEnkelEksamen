# FIE402 Corporate Finance · Mock exam 12

180 minutes · 100 points · 6 exercises · no multiple choice · calculator and one bilingual dictionary permitted. The formula sheet is handed out. Show every calculation.

## Exercise 1: Options (12 points)

Halsnøy Halvleder ASA trades at NOK 200 and has never paid a dividend. Consider European calls and puts on its shares with strike NOK 200 and two years to expiry. Treat each part separately; no calculations, 2-3 sentences per part (3 points each).

a) Ingrid expects the share to become far more volatile than the market expects, with no view on its direction. If the market comes round to her view while the share is still at NOK 200, which option gains more?

b) Are five-year European options with the same strike worth more than the two-year call and put?

c) A colleague says: «A higher risk-free rate discounts the payoffs more, so it lowers both option values.» Is he right if the share price is unchanged?

d) Halsnøy will pay out NOK 20 per share next month, as a dividend or as a repurchase at the market price. Which method would a call holder prefer and which a put holder?

## Exercise 2: Governance (12 points)

Corwin Instruments Inc., a US-listed firm with dispersed ownership, has lagged its peers for three years. Halberd Partners, a private equity firm holding 9% of the shares, has launched a hostile tender offer at a 30% premium. Corwin's board considers three responses:

(1) a poison pill adopted without a shareholder vote;  
(2) a white knight: a friendly bidder that would keep the managers;  
(3) greenmail: buying Halberd's stake at a premium if it drops the bid.

For each: how does it work against the bid? Who is likely to gain and who to lose among the managers, the other shareholders and Halberd? 2-3 sentences and 4 points each.

## Exercise 3: Capital Structure (18 points)

Lind Havbruk AS and Selje Havbruk AS each own one identical salmon farm and nothing else. At t = 1 each sells its farm and is wound up; a farm fetches NOK 205 million if salmon prices are high and NOK 125 million if low (50% each). Lind has no debt; Selje owes NOK 63 million (face value) on a zero-coupon loan due at t = 1. Markets are perfect and everyone can borrow and lend at 5%. Lind's equity trades at NOK 150 million, Selje's at NOK 96 million.

**a) (3 points)** What do each firm's shareholders receive at t = 1 in each state and what is Selje's loan worth today?

**b) (3 points)** What are the two firms worth and what expected return does each firm's equity offer?

**c) (4 points)** With NOK 9.6 million, Kari can buy 10% of Selje's shares, or 10% of Lind's shares with the rest borrowed at 5% in her own name. What does each plan pay at t = 1 in each state and which is better?

**d) (4 points)** Construct an arbitrage that needs no money of your own, trading 10% of each firm's shares. What is your profit and at what value of Selje's equity would the arbitrage disappear?

**e) (4 points)** Which theorem does this illustrate? State it and the assumptions about investors that your strategy in d) relies on. (3-4 sentences)

## Exercise 4: Debt and risk (18 points)

Leirvåg Gruppen ASA has two divisions, Havn (ports) and Seismikk (seismic surveys). It will be wound up at t = 1, when they produce these cash flows (NOK million):

| State (probability) | Bust (25%) | Normal (50%) | Boom (25%) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Havn | 140 | 150 | 160 |
| Seismikk | 20 | 110 | 260 |

Its only debt is a zero-coupon bond with face value NOK 150 million due at t = 1. Investors are risk neutral, the risk-free rate is 5% and there are no taxes. Use 4 decimal places.

**a) (3 points)** What are Leirvåg's bond and equity worth today?

**b) (6 points)** The CEO proposes to split Leirvåg into Havn ASA and Seismikk ASA, giving each shareholder one share in each. The bond terms let him move the whole bond to either firm. The split costs synergies: each firm's cash flow falls by NOK 6 million in every state. Which firm should take the bond if the CEO acts for the shareholders? Find their total equity value under both choices.

**c) (3 points)** For the CEO's choice, by what percentage do the value of the bond and the shareholders' total wealth change when the plan is announced?

**d) (3 points)** Who wins and who loses from the split and by how much? Does it create value?

**e) (3 points)** In the long run, who bears the cost of such transactions and are covenants that forbid them good or bad for shareholders? (2-3 sentences)

## Exercise 5: Valuation (20 points)

Kjølvik Lager AS, a cold-storage firm, expects for Year 1 (NOK million): EBIT 500, depreciation 80, capital expenditure 120 and an increase in net working capital of 20, all growing by 2.5% a year forever. The tax rate is 22%. Kjølvik has NOK 2,500 million of risk-free debt and keeps its debt-to-equity ratio constant, so the debt is expected to grow by 2.5% a year; interest is paid at year end on the opening debt. The risk-free rate is 4%, the expected market return 9% and the asset beta 0.8. Use 4 decimal places.

**a) (3 points)** Find Kjølvik's free cash flow in Year 1 and its unlevered value.

**b) (5 points)** Find the interest tax shield in Year 1 and the present value of all interest tax shields. Which rate do you use and why?

**c) (3 points)** Find Kjølvik's levered value with APV and the value of its equity.

**d) (2 points)** What after-tax WACC is consistent with the free cash flow and the levered value in c)?

**e) (3 points)** Find Kjølvik's cost of equity and equity beta. Do its equity and debt betas, weighted by value, average to its asset beta?

**f) (4 points)** Find the free cash flow to equity in Year 1 and value the equity with the flow-to-equity method. Compare with c).

## Exercise 6: Real Options (20 points)

Kobbervik Gruve AS can expand its copper mine by 3,000 tonnes a year forever for USD 200 million. The extra copper costs USD 7,000 per tonne to produce and is sold at each year end at that year's price. At t = 1 the copper price becomes USD 11,000 or USD 7,500 per tonne and stays there forever; nobody knows the probabilities. The risk-free rate is 4%.

Listed Nordmalm Royalty ASA, worth USD 245 million, owns only the right to 1,000 tonnes of copper a year forever from t = 1; it sells each year's copper at that year's price and pays out at once.

**a) (3 points)** What is the NPV of expanding now (output from Year 1)? Value the sales with Nordmalm's market value.

**b) (5 points)** Instead, Kobbervik can decide at t = 1, when the price is known, paying USD 200 million then; output starts in Year 2. What is the NPV of expanding in each state, measured at t = 1? Above which price does Kobbervik expand?

**c) (8 points)** What is the risk-neutral probability of the high price? Explain briefly why true probabilities are not needed.

**d) (4 points)** Without expanding now, what is Kobbervik's right to expand at t = 1 worth today? Do you expand now or wait? Briefly comment.
