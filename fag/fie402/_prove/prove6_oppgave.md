# FIE402 Corporate Finance · Mock exam 6

180 minutes · 100 points · 6 exercises · no multiple choice · calculator and one bilingual dictionary permitted. The formula sheet is handed out. Show every calculation.

## Exercise 1: M&A (12 points)

On the announcement of a takeover, target shareholders earn abnormal returns of about 15% on average, while acquirer shareholders earn about 1% and lose in about half of the deals.

a) Give two reasons why the target's shareholders capture most of the gains.

b) Give two reasons why a hostile takeover can increase the value of the target.

Reasons must be other than «synergies». The two reasons in each part must differ. Keep explanations short, 1-2 sentences per reason (3 points each).

## Exercise 2: Governance (12 points)

An index fund owns 0.1-0.5% of each of 2,000 listed companies: about 30,000 ballot items a year. Its governance team of four votes on every item as a proxy advisor (such as ISS or Glass Lewis) recommends.

a) Is this optimal for the fund's investors? Give one argument for, one against and your conclusion (6 points; 2-3 sentences per argument).

b) Propose one alternative way for the fund to discipline managers: who acts, how it works and one weakness (6 points; 3-4 sentences).

## Exercise 3: Capital Structure (18 points)

Skarvik Robotics ASA operates in a perfect capital market with no taxes. It has 60 million shares trading at NOK 50 and NOK 1,500 million of risk-free debt. Its assets generate expected EBIT of NOK 450 million per year forever, all paid out as interest and dividends. The risk-free rate is 4%, the expected market return is 9% and Skarvik's equity beta is 1.8.

**a) (3 points)** Find Skarvik's asset beta and unlevered cost of capital.

**b) (3 points)** Skarvik announces that it will issue new shares for NOK 1,000 million and use the proceeds to repay debt; the debt stays risk-free. What happens to the share price on announcement? How many new shares are issued?

**c) (4 points)** Find the equity beta and the cost of equity after the issue. Why do they change in this direction?

**d) (4 points)** Find the WACC before and after the issue. Explain the result using MM.

**e) (4 points)** A colleague objects: «Equity costs far more than the 4% we pay on debt, so replacing debt with equity must raise our cost of capital. Besides, the earnings are now shared among more shares, so EPS falls from NOK 6.50 and the existing shareholders are diluted.» Is the colleague right? Explain briefly (3-5 sentences).

## Exercise 4: Debt and risk (18 points)

Brattøy Verft AS, a shipyard, has a bank loan with face value NOK 50 million due at t = 1. Management, acting for the shareholders, plans to build a prototype vessel on speculation (Plan P): at t = 1 the yard's assets will be worth NOK 120 million if a buyer is found (probability 60%) and NOK 20 million if not (probability 40%). Investors are risk neutral and the risk-free rate is 0%. If the yard cannot repay the loan, the bank takes over its assets.

**a) (3 points)** Assume default is costless. Find the values of the loan, the equity and the firm today.

**b) (4 points)** In default, the assets must be sold in a fire sale for half their value. Recalculate the three values. With the loan already outstanding, who bears the cost of the fire sale? What are the loan's promised yield and expected return?

**c) (4 points)** Instead of Plan P, the Navy offers a fixed-price contract (Plan N) that makes the assets worth NOK 85 million at t = 1 for sure. Which plan would the yard choose without debt? Which plan does management choose with the loan, given the fire sale? Why?

**d) (4 points)** Find the agency cost of the loan and split it into the part due to Plan P's lower expected asset value and the part due to the fire sale. Who wins and who loses when P is chosen over N?

**e) (3 points)** The bank priced the loan a year ago, knowing that management would choose the plan later. Who bears the agency cost in the end? Name one way to avoid it (2-3 sentences).

## Exercise 5: Valuation (20 points)

Lofotkabel AS, an unlisted fibre-network operator, expects EBIT of NOK 500 million next year. Capital expenditure equals depreciation, working capital is constant and free cash flow grows by 2.5% per year forever. The tax rate is 22%. Lofotkabel's debt trades at par at NOK 2,000 million today; both its cost of debt and its interest rate are 5.5%. The firm keeps its debt-to-equity ratio constant, so the debt also grows by 2.5% per year. The risk-free rate is 4% and the expected market return is 9%.

Two comparable listed firms keep their debt-to-value ratios constant:

| Comparable | Cost of equity | Cost of debt | Leverage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nordkabel ASA | 11% | 5% | D/V = 30% |
| Vestnett ASA | 10% | 4% | D/E = 0.25 |

Use the average of their unlevered costs of capital. Recommended precision is 4 decimal places.

**a) (4 points)** Find Lofotkabel's unlevered cost of capital and asset beta.

**b) (3 points)** What would Lofotkabel be worth if it were all-equity financed?

**c) (3 points)** Find next year's interest and the present value of the interest tax shields. Which rate do you discount them at and why?

**d) (3 points)** Find Lofotkabel's levered value with the APV method, the value of its equity and its debt-to-value ratio.

**e) (3 points)** Find the after-tax WACC (hint: work backwards from the free cash flow and V^L). Then use the WACC formula to find the cost of equity.

**f) (4 points)** Find the equity and debt betas and show that their weighted average equals the asset beta. Briefly explain why r_wacc is lower than r_U.

## Exercise 6: Real Options (20 points)

Your licence to a small oil field expires after next year. You can extract all the oil now or next year. The quick method extracts 3 million barrels at USD 40 per barrel; the careful method extracts 5 million barrels at USD 55 per barrel. The oil is sold at the spot price when extracted and the cost is paid at the same time.

Oil trades at USD 70 today and will rise by 25% or fall by 20% over the year. Analysts put the probability of a rise at 60%. Oil is a traded asset that costs nothing to hold and pays nothing. The risk-free rate is 5%. Recommended precision is 4 decimal places.

**a) (2 points)** If you extract now, which method do you use? What is the field worth if you extract now?

**b) (3 points)** Set up the binomial tree for the oil price. Find the value of each method (oil minus extraction cost) at each end-point.

**c) (4 points)** At which oil price are the two methods equally good? Which method is optimal at each end-point? Why does the careful method win at high prices?

**d) (8 points)** What is the present value today of extracting next year? Use the risk-neutral probability implied by the oil price. Verify with a portfolio of oil and risk-free bonds that replicates the payoff.

**e) (3 points)** Do you extract now or wait? What is the option to wait worth? Explain why the analysts' 60% was not needed in d) and what goes wrong if you use it (2-3 sentences).
