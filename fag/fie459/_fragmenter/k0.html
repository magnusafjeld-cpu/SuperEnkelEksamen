0 · The course and the exam

FIE459 is graded on a closed-book exam of multiple-choice and true/false questions (70%) and a group report (30%). Both reward the same skill: telling apart concepts that sound alike and applying them to a short case. This chapter sets out what is assessed and how to use the rest of this manual.

0.1 How the course is built

Thirteen lectures in three parts, following the textbook, Schoenmaker & Schramade (2019), Principles of Sustainable Finance. Each chapter here covers one lecture.

0.2 The exam

  • 3-hour digital school exam, closed book, 16 December 2026, 70% of the grade.
  • Multiple-choice and true/false questions, answered in English.
  • The lecturer (Jiaying Li) puts the focus on «economic intuition, not calculation» and warns that memorising definitions is not the best strategy. Instead, attend, discuss, and relate news stories to the course concepts.
How a true/false statement is built A false statement is usually correct except for one swapped element: the wrong side of a distinction (outside-in for inside-out, action for measurement, moral hazard for adverse selection), or a qualifier such as «always» or «only». Read every clause. One wrong word makes the whole statement false.

0.3 The group project

Groups of five (sign up on Canvas before 1 October), deadline 12 November, at most 5 pages plus one page of references. Your group acts as equity analysts for a manager with a sustainability mandate. You choose one of five industries (retail and consumer goods; food and beverages; transport and mobility; energy and utilities; housing, buildings and construction), adopt one strategy from Lecture 8 (exclusionary screening, best-in-class screening, thematic investing, ESG integration or stewardship) and recommend five listed stocks with weights summing to 100%, no short selling. It must work financially and on sustainability grounds. The last question comes from Lecture 9: is the sustainability story already priced?

No technical work is required: a simple, well-supported analysis can earn full marks, and equal weights are acceptable if justified.

Past returns are not expected returns The marking scheme penalises «claiming that sustainable stocks always outperform/underperform» and using past returns as proof of future returns. Green stocks can earn high realised returns because good news arrived unexpectedly; that says little about expected returns from today's price (chapter 9).

0.4 How to use this manual

Read chapter 1 first: its Schoenmaker & Schramade table (finance as usual, SF 1.0, 2.0, 3.0) returns in chapters 5, 8 and 9. In every chapter, the mistake boxes are the traps a true/false question is built from, and the mech boxes give the mechanism you need for an unfamiliar case. After each chapter, take its quiz and review the flashcards. Chapters chapters 10–13 were written before their lectures and are marked provisional.

What the exam rewards Distinctions and applications, not recalled numbers. For every concept, ask: what is it most easily confused with, and what separates the two?
